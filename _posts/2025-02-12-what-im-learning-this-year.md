## Notes From Christianity and Western Culture Class - 2016 
I begin with a thought that I came across in one of my notebooks. It's a note from January 20, 2016. It comes directly from one of my professors at Moody Bible Institute, named Dr. McDuffie. When I came across it, I remembered that everything that Dr. McDuffie said in class seemed so profound to me that I took to just jotting down verbatum notes in his class. I was trying to capture the essence of what he was saying. Here's a brief snippet of what he said in one of his classes:

"How does modern culture view history?

The hallmark of a modern person is to view the present as superior to the past.  This causes us to lose interest in history.  We rely on the expertise of the professional producers of knowledge, in order to know anything.  We don’t listen to our grandparents or to those older than us to gain knowledge.  

We focus on the areas of knowledge that afford us the future of a career.  This is why we don’t find value in reading a history book or reading a novel. A history book.  Is there anything more useless? How do you study a historical text?

To read a history book. You imagine it to be very important. Create a scenario. Imagine 40,000 people who need to know the information in your textbook, and you are the only one with the book.

This isn’t Mark Twain, this is serious.

One thing more boring than history is law. The only reason people study law is because they can make a lot of money.

Read the outline of the book first. Read it out loud. 

Reading a book without caring about it is like walking into your grandmother’s living room with dirty shoes. “What’d you got? Sure I can read it!"

Don’t just read the text. Interact with it. Make it come alive. Make the words jump right out of your skull.  

Read the outline. Read the headings and pictures and sidebars of every chapter.

Know how to absorb a wide swath of information. Learn how to condense the information into a concise outline. Understand how to digest information

You want me to have enough sanity to remember my wife’s name when I get home. We’re not going to go on and on about this. That’s the way it’s going to work.

The Church in the Modern World of Its Making.

if you sunbath in the sandy beaches of your mind, that’s not my concern.  I want you to pay attention. soak it all in. drink deeply

There is blessing and there is bane.  There is good and there is bad that has come out of Latin Christianity.  

The Byzantine Empire was stable.  As stable as Egypt or China.  The West, however, was not stable. 

Migration. Language. Political instability.  Tension between the Emperor and the Papacy. This instability that is the progenitor of modernity

The protect the stability of the papacy and prevent lay oversight, the professional production of knowledge and reason is harnessed and centralized in the form of the university.  Rational arguments to secure papal authority.  Out of this context, we get modern culture."
